## Case

Emily Jane is 5 weeks old and her mother Melinda is concerned because she is making a noisy breathing noise.


You note that Emily's breathing is noisier when she lies flat and that it improves when you place her in a more upright position.


When you ask Melinda to describe the noise, she is not sure whether it occurs during inspiration or expiration, but says that it sounds "high pitched".


Emily is feeding well and has normal growth and development. There is no history of choking or breathing difficulties.

<CaseQuestion
  number={2}
  question="Emily's mother describes the noise as a high pitched 'squeak', you determine that this occurs during inspiration (stridor). Briefly outline the causes of stridor in infants and what features on history and clinical examination would help you narrow down the differential diagnosis."
>

**Definition:**
        

Stridor is a harsh, high-pitched respiratory sound that is usually heard during inspiration. It indicates partial obstruction of the airway, usually at the level of the larynx or trachea.

        

**Common Causes of Stridor in Infants:**

        <table>
          <tr>
            <th>Cause</th>
            <th>Key Features</th>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Foreign Body**</td>
            <td>Sudden onset, history of choking, may have unilateral wheeze, variable presentation depending on location</td>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Anaphylaxis**</td>
            <td>Acute onset, associated with exposure to allergen, urticaria, angioedema, respiratory distress, cardiovascular compromise</td>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Croup (Laryngotracheobronchitis)**</td>
            <td>Viral infection, barking cough, fever, preceded by coryzal symptoms, worse at night, age 6 months - 3 years</td>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Vocal Cord Paralysis**</td>
            <td>Present from birth, weak cry, aspiration, may be bilateral or unilateral</td>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Laryngomalacia**</td>
            <td>Most common cause of stridor in infants, present from first few weeks, positional (worse when supine), improves with growth, feeding usually normal</td>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Burns/Inhalation Injury**</td>
            <td>History of fire, smoke exposure, chemical exposure, facial burns, singed nasal hairs</td>
          </tr>
          <tr>
            <td>**Epiglottitis**</td>
            <td>Rare since Hib vaccination, acute onset, high fever, toxic appearance, drooling, reluctance to lie flat (tripod position), medical emergency</td>
          </tr>
        </table>

        

**Other Causes:** Subglottic stenosis, laryngeal web, vascular ring, hemangioma, bacterial tracheitis

        

**Red Flags Requiring Urgent Assessment:**
        

          - Acute onset stridor

          - Severe respiratory distress (tachypnea, retractions, cyanosis)

          - Drooling or difficulty swallowing

          - Toxic appearance or high fever

          - History of choking or foreign body

          - Rapidly progressive symptoms

</CaseQuestion>
