WK1 - Anaesthesia Learning Objectives

Pre-operative Assessment (Low Risk)
Know the key components of history and examination when assessing a patient for whom a general anaesthetic is planned for an elective procedure.[ie low risk]
Understand the importance of assessing exercise tolerance in patients prior to a general anaesthetic.
Know how to assess exercise tolerance in a patient for whom a general anaesthetic is planned.
Outline the investigations that are required as part of the assessment for a patient for whom a general anaesthetic is planned for an elective procedure and be able to explain the rationale for these investigations.
Interpret the results of investigations in the context of patient requiring a general anaesthetic and be able to appropriately modify an anaesthetic plan as a result of investigation results.
Pre-operative Assessment (High Risk) & Balanced Anaesthesia
Know the key components of the assessment of a patient for general anaesthetic with a recent history of severe illness. [ie high risk]
Outline the investigations that are required as part of the assessment of a high risk patient for whom a general anaesthetic and be able to explain the rationale for these investigations.
Define general anaesthesia.
Detail the components of 'balanced general anaesthesia'
Explain the term minimum alveolar concentration [MAC] in relation to inhalational anaesthetic agents.
Outline the factors that can increase and decrease MAC.
Explain the mechanism of action, pharmacokinetics, possible adverse reaction and drug interactions of the following inhalational anaesthetics – Isoflurane, sevoflurane, desflurane and nitrous oxide.
Explain the mechanism of action, pharmacokinetics, possible adverse reaction and drug interactions of the following intravenous anaesthetic agents' propofol, thiopentone and ketamine & midazolam.
Explain the mechanism of action, pharmacokinetics, possible adverse reaction and drug interactions of the following opiods, morphine, fentanyl and Alfentanil and non opiod tramadol.
Explain the mechanism of action, pharmacokinetics, possible adverse reaction and drug interactions of the following muscle relaxants, suxemethonium, Rocuronium, Vecuronium, cisatracurium.
Airway Management & Special Considerations
Know the key components of the assessment of a patient for general anaesthetic with a history of respiratory illness eg asthma.
Outline the peri-operative management plan of patients with reactive airways disease who require general anaesthesia.
Outline the factors that need be considered when obese patients require general anaesthesia.
Know the anatomy of the airway from the oral and nasal cavities to the trachea, including the sensory innervations of upper and lower airway.
Outline the key steps in preparation for airway management prior to intubation for general anaesthesia in an elective procedure.
Know the key equipment that is used in airway management and be able to identify and briefly outline the use and function of the following equipment endotracheal tube, oral airway, nasopharyngeal airway, laryngeal mask, bougie, stylet and magill forceps.
Know the indications for endotracheal intubation.
Know the potential complications of endotracheal intubation.
Outline the process for confirming correct placement of the endotracheal tube.
Emergency Anaesthesia
Know the key components of the history and examination in the assessment of a trauma patient for an emergency general anaesthetic.
Outline the key features that contribute to aspiration risk in a patient undergoing an emergency general anaesthetic.
Outline the key features that contribute to potentially difficult airway management in a patient undergoing an emergency general anaesthetic and how to optimise patient preparation.
Describe the principles of a rapid sequence induction for an emergency general anaesthetic.
Questions

WK1 - Pre-operative Assessment SAQs

Question 1 of 5
Outline the key components of the history and physical examination when assessing a low-risk patient for an elective general anaesthetic.
Your Answer:
Model Answer:
History
  • Past anaesthetic history (e.g. PONV, difficult airway, MH)
  • Medical comorbidities (cardiac, respiratory, renal, endocrine)
  • Medications (anticoagulants, antihypertensives, insulin, supplements)
  • Allergies and adverse drug reactions
  • Smoking, alcohol, recreational drugs
  • Fasting status
  • Functional capacity
  • Recent illness (URTI, COVID)
Examination
  • Airway assessment (Mallampati, mouth opening, neck mobility, dentition)
  • Cardiovascular exam (BP, pulse, murmurs)
  • Respiratory exam (wheeze, crackles, work of breathing)
  • BMI and general habitus
  • Baseline observations
Question 2 of 5
Explain why assessment of exercise tolerance is important prior to a general anaesthetic.
Your Answer:
Model Answer:
  • Reflects cardiopulmonary reserve
  • Predicts perioperative cardiac and respiratory risk
  • Poor tolerance associated with increased perioperative morbidity
  • Guides need for further investigation
  • Assists in anaesthetic risk stratification
Question 3 of 5
Describe how exercise tolerance should be assessed in a patient planned for an elective general anaesthetic.
Your Answer:
Model Answer:
  • Functional history using METs-based activities
  • ≥4 METs: climbing 2 flights of stairs, brisk walking
  • <4 METs: difficulty with stairs, breathless at rest or minimal exertion
  • Poor exercise tolerance may warrant further assessment depending on context
Question 4 of 5
Outline the investigations required for a low-risk patient undergoing elective general anaesthesia and explain the rationale.
Your Answer:
Model Answer:
  • No routine investigations in asymptomatic low-risk patients
  • Investigations guided by history and examination
  • FBC: suspected anaemia, major surgery
  • U&E: renal disease, ACE-I/ARB or diuretic use
  • ECG: known CVD, significant risk factors or symptoms
  • Pregnancy test: if pregnancy possible
  • BGL/HbA1c: known or suspected diabetes
  • Rationale: avoid unnecessary testing and delays
Question 5 of 5
A patient's pre-operative investigations reveal abnormalities. Describe how these findings may alter the anaesthetic plan.
Your Answer:
Model Answer:
  • Anaemia: optimise haemoglobin, consider delaying elective surgery
  • Renal impairment: dose-adjust drugs, avoid nephrotoxins, manage fluids carefully
  • Cardiac abnormalities: further evaluation, increased monitoring, consider regional techniques
  • Poor glycaemic control: optimise pre-op, perioperative glucose monitoring
  • Respiratory disease: pre-op optimisation, consider postoperative HDU monitoring
